12 questions to ask your heat pump installer

Ask these in writing. A good installer will be glad you did. Maryland wording is shown. DC and Virginia notes follow the list.

  1. Participating contractor? Are you on my utility's participating contractor list (Home Performance, HVAC or both)? Which list? Check the utility's list yourself.
  2. Which rebate track? Are we using HVAC, Home Performance or Switch-to-Electric? Why that one?
  3. Which date? Will the install be finished by Nov 30, 2026 (Pepco/Delmarva switching amounts) or reserved by Dec 31, 2026 (Pepco Home Performance)? What happens to my price if it slips?
  4. Line items. Will you list each incentive on the contract as its own line?
  5. Equipment. What is the AHRI number? Does the unit meet the program tier's SEER2 and HSPF2 (and cold-climate) minimums?
  6. Load calculation. Did you do a room-by-room Manual J after the air sealing and insulation plan? Can I have a copy?
  7. Testing. Will you test with a blower door and test the ducts, before and after?
  8. Licenses. What are your license numbers? Check them yourself: {{LICENSE_MD}} · {{LICENSE_DC}} · {{LICENSE_VA}}.
  9. Permit. Will you pull the permit?
  10. Federal credit. Are you counting on any federal tax credit or federal rebate? In 2026, the right answer is no. The IRS 25C credit ended for heat pumps placed in service after Dec 31, 2025.
  11. Electrical work. Do I need "make-ready" electrical work or a panel upgrade? Which program pays for it?
  12. Paperwork. Who sends in the rebate paperwork? When will I see the money?
In DC: ask for the installer's DC Master Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Mechanic license, and the DOB mechanical permit. Take before and after photos showing the gas line. Apply within 30 days of the install.
In Northern Virginia: ask whether they're a Dominion Home Energy Evaluation participating contractor, and whether the auditor holds a BPI Building Analyst certification. Dominion does not pay for switching from gas.

The envelope-first order

  1. Energy audit. Learn how your home uses energy.
  2. Air sealing. Often with a blower door test.
  3. Insulation. Keep the heat where you want it.
  4. The switch. A heat pump sized with a Manual J, after the fixes.
